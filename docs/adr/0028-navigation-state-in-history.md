# Keep the client's navigation state in the address and on the history entry

A screen's address is the state that restores it. A Digest address carries the same `from` and `through` parameters as its API request, written and read by one encoding (`shared/api.ts` `digestParamsOf` and `digestRequestSchema`), and a search address carries the query, the Search Scope and the sort (`searchParamsOf` and `searchRequestSchema`). Restoring an address therefore restores the same request, which is how the way back to the Digest returns to the day it left (ADR 0011). What an address cannot hold goes on its history entry, read through `routing.ts` `historyStateSchema`: `origin`, the way back out of a nested screen as a trail of at most six addresses, each with the label its Back button shows; `beneath`, the address of the entry under this one, recorded when the app pushed it; `scrollY`, recorded when the app pushes past the entry; and `kept`, what a screen keeps of how it was left. Because these live in `history.state` rather than in memory, they survive Back, Forward and reload. A trail that fails to parse falls back to the screen's own section.

`routing.ts` `returnTo` compares the way back with `beneath`. When the way back is the entry underneath, the app calls `history.back()` instead of pushing, so the app's Back and the browser's Back are one move that lands on the same entry, scrolled where it was left (`docs/DESIGN.md` §5 Back button). Otherwise it pushes the way back as a new entry. Opening a section, a Feed or the Reader View pushes an entry. Picking another Digest day, stepping to the next item in the Reader View, and typing, scoping or sorting a search replace the current entry, so Back leaves the screen rather than replaying each step. After an unsubscribe the Feed screen goes to Feeds, not to its way back, because the way back can point at an article of the Feed just left.

A screen keeps a value on its history entry with `useKept`, so coming back to the entry finds the screen as it was left: the oldest Digest day shown (`through`, ADR 0011), which Feed's box stands unfolded on a day, which Feed chip is pressed. A pushed entry keeps nothing yet, and an entry replaced by another address loses what it kept. Kept values are primitives read back through a schema because `useSyncExternalStore` compares snapshots by identity: an object parsed fresh on every read never compares equal to the last one, and the screen would render forever.

The search line commits its words 250ms after typing pauses (`components/global-search.tsx` `SETTLE_MS`). A commit replaces the history entry and fetches results, and Safari throws once a page writes history about a hundred times in thirty seconds, so the pause also keeps fast typing under that limit. The line is keyed by the address its search belongs to (`searchHomeOf`), so on a phone moving to another screen closes it whatever it held. Words taken away from outside the line, as by going back from the results, close it too, even when that lands on the screen the search began from; clearing the words inside the line keeps it open.
