# Place every day in the installation timezone on the server, and send it as a day key

Which calendar day a Feed Item falls on is decided by the server, in the installation timezone, and sent to the client as a day key such as `2026-08-08`. The Digest's days, the Cadence of a Feed, a Search result's date, the day a Feed last answered and the days of a Feed preview (today, its newest Feed Item's day and each listed Feed Item's day) are all day keys. In the contract a day key is `DateKey` (`src/shared/api.ts`), a branded string made only by parsing with `dateKeySchema`, by `utcDayKey`, or by the server's `dateKey` (`src/server/calendar.ts`), so a bare string or an instant cannot stand in for one on either side. A Feed preview names each Feed Item's day by its chronology (ADR 0011), as the Feed will once subscribed, so an undated or future-dated Feed Item shows the day it was previewed. A day key is a date, not an instant, so the client reads and writes it in UTC (`src/client/day-names.ts` formats it with `timeZone: 'UTC'`) and no timezone, the browser's included, can move it to a neighbouring day. The alternative, sending instants and letting each browser group them, was rejected because one installation would then group the same Feed Items differently on a laptop and a phone set to different timezones, where the installation timezone exists to make the Digest the same on every device.

The server converts many stored instants to day keys at once, for a whole page or a whole Feed, so it does not ask `Intl` for each one. `dayKeysIn` (`src/server/calendar.ts`) takes the instant's UTC date as a first guess and settles it against the UTC instants where that day and the next begin in the installation timezone, cached per day. Every UTC offset is under a day, so the answer is always the UTC date or one of its neighbours, and `Intl` runs once per day touched rather than once per item. `dayStartUtc` finds the instant a day begins by trying midnight under the offset in force on either side of the day and keeping the one that is that day's first instant. Where a daylight-saving transition skips midnight, the day begins at the transition itself. `tests/server/calendar.test.ts` pins this across half-hour and quarter-hour offsets, both sides of the date line, and zones whose transition falls at midnight.

Counting Feed Items per day stays in SQL. `countRows` (`src/server/digest/cadence-window.ts`) turns each day into its opening and closing instants in a `VALUES` table and counts with one range over the indexed `chronology_at` per day (ADR 0011), so no row crosses into JavaScript. It joins the days to `feed_items` with `CROSS JOIN`, which in SQLite fixes the join order to the order written. Without statistics the planner may otherwise put the days in the inner loop and walk every item of every Feed once per day.
